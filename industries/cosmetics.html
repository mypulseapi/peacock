Suitability decides the sale, and suitability is not a field

Personal care is bought against a specific need: a skin type, a concern, an ingredient to seek out or avoid. Those are exactly the attributes most catalogues hold as prose, or print on the back of a bottle, which is why a shopper who knows precisely what they want still cannot narrow the shelf. Nobody has to write the fact down. Somebody has to make it findable.

What usually goes wrong in Cosmetics and personal care

What a buyer here is trying to decide:

Is this suitable for my skin and what I am trying to fix?

  • Skin type and concern held as marketing prose rather than structured values

  • Ingredient information present but unsearchable, which matters for allergies

  • Shade and finish ranges recorded inconsistently across brands

  • Repeat purchases lost because the catalogue cannot answer a suitability question

Where it usually already lives: Ingredient lists and supplier data you already hold for compliance. The audit usually finds this is a structuring job rather than an acquisition one.

The same shelf, once the top gap is closed

Before Pulse

  • –A buyer asking “Is this suitable for my skin and what I am trying to fix?” has no way to narrow this shelf.
  • –Concern addressed decides the purchase and is recorded on about 24% of the catalogue, so the filter either does not exist or hides most of your range.
  • –Enrichment gets quoted as a whole-catalogue project, because nobody can say which field to start with.

After the repair

  • The same buyer narrows on Concern addressed and reaches a shortlist they can choose from.
  • Concern addressed came first on the ranked list, measured against what buyers in this sector actually ask, not against how much data was missing.
  • The remaining gaps are still ranked, so the next round of work is already ordered and costed.
