Nobody decides to let a category decay. It happens because a supplier changes their export format, or a buyer onboards a new range in a hurry, or the person who understood the taxonomy left. Each of those adds products that skip the fields your shoppers rely on.

By the time anyone notices, the shelf has been quietly worse for a quarter and you are back where you started, except now the enrichment work has already been done once and asking for it again is a harder conversation.

One shelf fell from 71 to 62 because 340 new SKUs arrived with the deciding field blank. That is the sentence you want, in the week it happens, not at the quarterly review.

How it works

Set it once, then hear about it when it matters.

  1. 01 Once

    Set the cadence

    Weekly, monthly or on a feed. It reads the same export the audit read, so there is nothing new to build and no integration to maintain.

  2. 02 Ongoing

    We re-measure

    The same method on every run, so the numbers are comparable over time rather than a series of unrelated snapshots.

  3. 03 When it matters

    You get told, with the reason

    Not a dashboard you have to remember to open. An alert naming the shelf, the attribute and what caused the drop.

What you get

Four things, and nothing to install.

  • An alert that names the cause

    Which category slipped, which attribute did it, and how many new products arrived without it. Enough to act on without opening anything.

  • A trend per category

    Readiness over time per shelf, so you can see the slow drift as well as the sudden drop, and argue for the fix before it is urgent.

  • A cadence you choose

    Some catalogues change daily and some change twice a year. You set how often, and you can change it.

  • Nothing new to maintain

    It reads the export you already produce. No tag, no integration, no library to keep up to date as your platform changes.

Questions

The ones that decide it.

How often should we run it?

Match it to how often your catalogue actually changes. A marketplace onboarding sellers weekly wants weekly. A specialist retailer with two seasonal ranges wants monthly or even quarterly. Running it more often than your catalogue changes just produces identical numbers.

Does Pulse need access to my database, servers, or website backend?

No. It reads the same export the audit read. If you can schedule that export, monitoring works, and nothing on your site or in your stack changes.

Can we monitor categories we have not audited?

They need an audit first to set the baseline. After that, monitoring keeps measuring against it.

Will we have technical support during the monitoring process?

Yes. Monitoring is a running engagement rather than a delivered file, so you keep the same contact through it: someone who knows your taxonomy, reads the alerts with you and helps decide which ones are worth acting on.

What customer systems and browser data can Pulse access, and what reaches Pulse beyond what we provide at integration?

Nothing beyond what you provide. Pulse only accesses the data explicitly supplied through the integration, plus anonymous browser signals where the optional runtime is installed. It does not reach into internal systems and it does not collect personally identifiable information.
