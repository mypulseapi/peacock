A completeness dashboard reports the same green number whether a shelf gained ten well-recorded products or ten blank ones.

So the decay is invisible until a buyer complains, a filter returns nothing, or someone asks why a category stopped converting and nobody can name the week it changed.

The ones that decide it

How often should we run it?

Match it to how often your catalogue actually changes. A marketplace onboarding sellers weekly wants weekly. A specialist retailer with two seasonal ranges wants monthly or even quarterly. Running it more often than your catalogue changes just produces identical numbers.

Does Pulse need access to my database, servers, or website backend?

No. It reads the same export the audit read. If you can schedule that export, monitoring works, and nothing on your site or in your stack changes.

Can we monitor categories we have not audited?

They need an audit first to set the baseline. After that, monitoring keeps measuring against it.

Will we have technical support during the monitoring process?

Yes. Monitoring is a running engagement rather than a delivered file, so you keep the same contact through it: someone who knows your taxonomy, reads the alerts with you and helps decide which ones are worth acting on.

What can Pulse see of our systems and our shoppers?

Only the export you send. Monitor reads the same category export the audit read: no integration, nothing installed on your site, no access to your internal systems, and no personal data about shoppers.
