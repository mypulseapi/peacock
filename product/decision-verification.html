The reporting problem

A quarter of work, and the dashboard agrees.

Enrichment projects have a reporting problem. The work is real, it takes a quarter, and at the end the only evidence is that some fields are fuller than they were. Nobody can tell whether the right fields got filled, and the person who approved the budget is entitled to ask.

The usual answer is to wait for traffic and hope conversion moved. That takes months, it is confounded by everything else you shipped, and a bad number still will not tell you whether the enrichment was wrong or the weather was.

You should not have to wait a quarter for traffic to tell you whether you filled in the right fields.

How it works

Re-export, re-measure, compare.

  1. 01 You

    Re-export the category

    The same shelf, with whatever your team managed to populate. It does not need to be finished; partial repairs measure fine and often tell you where to stop.

  2. 02 Us

    We run the same measurement

    Identical method, identical attributes, so the two runs are comparable. Nothing is re-tuned between them, which is the only way a before and after means anything.

  3. 03 Both

    You get the difference

    Coverage per attribute, how much of the gap closed, and where your readiness number landed. Written so it can be forwarded to whoever signed the budget.

  • An answer the same week, because this is computed from your catalogue rather than accumulated from traffic

Questions

The ones that decide it.

How soon after the repair can we verify?

Immediately. The measurement reads your catalogue, not your traffic, so there is nothing to wait for. Merchants usually verify the week the population work finishes, while the team still remembers what they did.

Does a partial repair still measure?

Yes, and it is often the more useful run. Populating 60% of a field and measuring tells you whether the remaining 40% is worth the effort, which is a question nobody can answer from a completeness percentage.

Can we verify a category you did not audit?

Not meaningfully. Verification is a comparison against a baseline, so the category needs an audit first to establish what it was.

Can Pulse be customized for different product categories?

Yes. Pulse adapts the product attributes, the buying criteria and the recommendations it produces to your specific product category, so a laptop shelf and a skincare shelf are not scored against the same questions.

Finished the repair? Send it back.

Same category, same measurement, and a document you can hand to whoever asked whether it was worth doing.
